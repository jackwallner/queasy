Best Motion Sickness App for iPhone

By Jack Wallner · Last updated October 18, 2026

Try first, free and already on your iPhone: Apple's Vehicle Motion Cues, which shows moving dots at the screen edges while you ride. Best if you want on-screen visual modes and a trip log for car rides: RideCalm. Best if you want a short audio session through headphones: Dizzout or Hearapy. Best if you want Apple Watch routines you run yourself, such as paced breathing and wrist taps: Trip Comfort, the app I make. None of these apps is a medical treatment, and most have very few ratings.

How we compared (October 13, 2026): I read each app's US App Store listing and Apple's support page for Vehicle Motion Cues, and pulled ratings, rating counts, supported devices, purchase types and update dates from Apple's store data. I did not install or subscribe to the other apps, so what I say about them comes from their own listings, including any claims they make. Trip Comfort's entry comes from its code and listing. Prices are left out on purpose, because they vary by region and change.

Disclosure: Jack Wallner makes Trip Comfort: Motion Sickness, one of the apps below, so read its entry with that in mind.

General information, not medical advice. Nothing on this page diagnoses, treats, cures or prevents any condition. If symptoms are severe or you cannot keep fluids down, talk to a doctor. Do not use a phone, headphones or any of these apps while driving.

Vehicle Motion Cues (built into iPhone)

Apple's own setting puts animated dots at the edges of the screen that move with the vehicle, without covering what you are reading. Turn it on in Settings, Accessibility, Motion, Vehicle Motion Cues, where Automatic shows the dots only when iPhone detects you are riding in a vehicle, or add it to Control Center. It costs nothing and needs no download, so it is the obvious first thing to try if screens are the trigger. Apple says not to use it while operating a vehicle.

RideCalm: Car Sickness Control

RideCalm uses your phone's motion sensors to drive three on-screen modes: a stable horizon line, dots that move against the car's motion, and a dark, low-distraction screen. It adds breathing exercises, a wrist acupressure guide, calming audio, a trip history and seat suggestions. Its listing shows no in-app purchases. The trade-off: it is built for car travel, it was last updated on July 7, 2026, and it has 21 ratings.

Dizzout - Stop Motion Sickness

Dizzout is an audio session you play through headphones, before a trip or once discomfort starts, and its listing pitches it for cars, taxis, trains, planes and boats. It is updated often, most recently on October 9, 2026. The trade-offs: the listing does not explain what the audio is, it is iPhone only, and it has 3 ratings.

Hearapy: Motion Sickness Aid

Hearapy plays a 60-second 100 Hz tone through AirPods, generated live, with matching taps from the Taptic Engine. It runs on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, and its listing says it is not a medical device. The trade-off: it does one thing, and it has a single rating so far.

Trip Comfort: Motion Sickness (my app)

Four modes you run yourself. Breathe paces your breathing with gentle haptics. Pulse is a regular wrist-tapping pattern to rest your attention on. Tone plays a one-minute 100 Hz tone on iPhone through headphones, from a 2025 Nagoya University paper whose title says the tone may improve motion sickness measures. Press guides you to the wrist point and times a three-minute hold you apply with your thumb or an existing band. Three quick questions suggest a mode, and the app shows its sources next to each one. All four modes are free; free Pulse and Breathe sessions last two minutes. Its limits: wrist taps are not acupressure or nerve stimulation, there is no on-screen motion aid, Pulse and Breathe on the wrist need an Apple Watch, it may not change how you feel, and it has no ratings yet.

Other apps you will see

Two more apps come up when you search for a motion sickness app. I did not install either; the notes below come from their store listings as of October 13, 2026.

Carsick.App - Motion Sickness

Carsick.App is for passengers who work on a laptop. The phone app reads the vehicle's motion and sends it to a companion app on your computer, which shows moving visual patterns on the laptop screen, and several computers can pair with one phone, per its listing. The listing says a subscription is required for the motion features to work, after a seven-day trial. It has a 2.64 rating from 14 ratings and was last updated May 4, 2025.

KineStop: Car sickness aid (Android only)

KineStop is an Android app from Urbandroid, and it is not on the iPhone App Store. Its Google Play listing says it draws an artificial horizon over whatever is on screen, so you can keep using your usual video player or e-reader in the car, and notes that iPhone now has a similar built-in option in Vehicle Motion Cues. It suits Android users who want something like that setting; on iPhone, Vehicle Motion Cues is already built in.

Which should you pick?

  • Screens make you queasy in the car: turn on Vehicle Motion Cues first.
  • You ride in cars a lot and want visual modes plus a log of what helps: RideCalm.
  • You want something to play through headphones before you set off: Dizzout or Hearapy.
  • You want a few drug-free things to try from your wrist with your eyes shut: Trip Comfort: Motion Sickness.
  • Symptoms are severe, or medicine is on the table: talk to a doctor or pharmacist, not an app.

Frequently asked questions

Does iPhone have a built-in motion sickness setting?

Yes. Vehicle Motion Cues shows animated dots at the edges of the screen that move with the vehicle. Turn it on in Settings, Accessibility, Motion, Vehicle Motion Cues, or add it to Control Center. Apple says not to use it while operating a vehicle.

What is the 100 Hz tone in motion sickness apps?

Hearapy and Trip Comfort play a 100 Hz tone through headphones. It comes from a 2025 Nagoya University paper whose title says one minute of the tone may improve motion sickness measures. That is one study, and an app that plays the tone is not a medical treatment.

Which motion sickness apps work on Apple Watch?

Trip Comfort and Hearapy support Apple Watch. Trip Comfort runs its Pulse and Breathe modes on the watch. RideCalm and Dizzout do not list Apple Watch support.

Can an app replace motion sickness medicine?

No app here is a medical treatment. Ask a doctor or pharmacist about medicines, and get medical advice if symptoms are severe or you cannot keep fluids down.

What is the best iPhone app for motion sickness?

Start with Apple's Vehicle Motion Cues, which is free, built into iPhone and aimed at reading or scrolling as a passenger. Among App Store apps, RideCalm offers on-screen visual modes and a car trip log, Dizzout and Hearapy play short audio sessions through headphones, and Trip Comfort runs breathing and wrist-tap routines on Apple Watch. None of them is a medical treatment, and most have very few ratings.

Does motion sickness on iPhone actually work?

Apple describes Vehicle Motion Cues as a feature that can help reduce motion sickness for passengers, not as a treatment. It shows animated dots at the edges of the screen that represent changes in the vehicle's motion, which Apple says is meant to reduce the conflict between what you see and what you feel without covering what you are reading. It costs nothing to try under Settings, Accessibility, Motion, and Apple says not to use it while operating a vehicle.
