## Four things to try when you feel sick

Trip Comfort is a drug-free motion sickness app for Apple Watch and iPhone. A steady tap on your wrist, a paced breath you feel rather than watch, a one-minute 100 Hz tone, and a timed hold on the spot an acupressure band sits on. All free and unlimited, no account.

## What are Trip Comfort's four modes?

Pulse, Breathe, Tone and Press. Named for what they actually do, with the evidence, or the lack of it, stated next to them.

### Pulse

A steady, unhurried tap on the inside of your wrist. Something to rest your attention on while a wave passes. Ten levels, adjustable without stopping.

### Breathe

Your watch taps a long swell in and a longer fade out, so you can pace your breathing with your eyes shut and your arm down.

### Tone

A pure 100 Hz tone for one minute, through headphones, at everyday volume. The frequency from the 2025 Nagoya University paper.

### Press

Shows you the spot three finger-widths below the wrist crease that an acupressure band sits on, then times a three-minute hold while you press.

### Screen-off sessions

Pulse, Breathe and Press keep running on your wrist with the display off, using watchOS extended runtime. No HealthKit, no workouts.

### Three questions

Say what set it off and how bad it is, and Trip Comfort suggests the mode with the most relevant published work behind it, at a level and length to match.

### Linked in every mode

Every mode shows what it leans on, in the source’s own words, with a link. Pulse is the one mode with nothing to cite, and it says so.

### No watch needed

Every mode runs on iPhone alone, through Core Haptics and the speaker, because plenty of people who get queasy don’t own a watch.

### Stays on your devices

Check-ins, sessions and settings never leave your iPhone and Apple Watch. No account, no ads, no tracking, and you can export the log for a doctor.

## Is Trip Comfort private?

Yes. Trip Comfort has no account and no sign-up. Your check-ins, sessions, and settings stay on your iPhone and Apple Watch. There are no ads and no tracking. Apple processes purchases, and RevenueCat receives only the identifiers needed to unlock and restore the Pro upgrade.

## More info

How it's built.

### The Pitch

Motion sickness can show up in the back seat, on a boat, in the morning, or in a waiting room, when reaching for your phone is usually the last thing you want to do.

Trip Comfort keeps its routines on your wrist. Start a mode in a tap, let it run with the screen off, and adjust it without looking. If the watch is at home, everything runs on the iPhone instead.

It is four small things. Nobody knows which of them will suit you, including us, so the app makes them all easy to try, tells you what each one leans on, and keeps a log so you can see which one you tend to come out of feeling better.

### If it’s morning sickness

Pregnancy sickness is one reason people look for an acupressure band, and it is rarely only mornings. Trip Comfort includes morning sickness as an occasion: choose it and the pulse starts gentler, the session runs longer, and the guidance stays practical.

An empty stomach is the usual trigger, so small and often beats three meals. Plain crackers before you sit up. Cold food rather than hot, because it carries less smell. Fluids between meals rather than with them. Acupressure bands are one of the drug-free things people try, which is what the Press mode is there to help you use properly, since most people wear the band in the wrong place.

**What an app cannot do:** if you cannot keep fluids down for a day, if you are losing weight, if you feel faint or your urine is very dark, call your midwife or doctor. Severe pregnancy sickness has a name, hyperemesis gravidarum, and it has treatment. Trip Comfort is not it, and it will tell you so in the app.

### What Mattered

**Screen-off first.** Something you have to stare at is useless in a moving car, so extended runtime and wrist-down operation came before any UI polish.

**Three taps, maximum.** Nobody wants a questionnaire when they feel awful. The check-in is three questions, and the mode cards skip even those.

**What it is not.** A watch tapping your wrist is not acupressure and not nerve stimulation. Those are different things, done by bands that apply pressure and by cleared devices that deliver current. Trip Comfort says that out loud, in the app and here, because the alternative is implying something it cannot back.

**No gate on the way in.** Asking a nauseated person for card details is both unkind and pointless. Everything works free; Pro buys length and memory.

**Watch and phone parity.** Every mode works without an Apple Watch, because plenty of people who get queasy don't own one.

## Questions

Quick answers about the motion sickness app.

## Is there a motion sickness app for Apple Watch?

Yes. Trip Comfort: Motion Sickness offers four drug-free comfort routines on Apple Watch, and Pulse, Breathe and Press can keep running on your wrist with the screen off. It is a set of techniques you run yourself and it may not change how you feel.

## What can I try in Trip Comfort when I feel sick on a trip?

There are four modes: Pulse, a steady tap on your wrist; Breathe, a paced breath; Tone, a one-minute 100 Hz sound; and Press, a timed hold on the spot an acupressure band sits on. A three-question check-in suggests one to start with.

## Is Trip Comfort free, and do I need an account?

Yes, all four modes are free and unlimited, and no account is needed. Free Pulse and Breathe sessions run two minutes, and the optional Pro upgrade adds longer sessions, full history, your own rhythm and Press reminders. Your check-ins and sessions stay on your iPhone and Apple Watch.

## Does the motion sickness app work without an Apple Watch?

Yes. Every mode runs on iPhone alone, using Core Haptics and the speaker.

## Is Trip Comfort a medicine or a medical device?

No. It is a set of drug-free comfort techniques, it is not a medical device and holds no regulatory clearance, and it does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent anything. If symptoms are severe or persistent, or you cannot keep fluids down, seek medical advice.

## What is the 100 Hz tone mode?

Tone plays a pure 100 Hz tone for one minute through headphones at everyday volume. The frequency comes from a 2025 Nagoya University paper, and the app links the source and says what the evidence does and does not show.

## Does the app do acupressure?

No. Press shows you the spot three finger-widths below the wrist crease where an acupressure band sits, then times a three-minute hold while you press with your own thumb or a band you already own. A watch tapping your wrist is not acupressure.

Trip Comfort is a set of drug-free comfort techniques you run yourself. It is not a medical device, it holds no regulatory clearance, and it does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent anything. It may not change how you feel. Acupressure bands and wrist stimulation devices sold for nausea are cleared medical devices; Trip Comfort is neither, and is not a substitute for one. Evidence for wrist pressure and sound-based techniques is mixed and varies by person, and the app says where. If nausea is severe or persistent, comes with other symptoms, or you can’t keep fluids down, talk to a doctor.
